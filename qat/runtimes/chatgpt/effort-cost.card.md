# Effort pocket card

**What one ChatGPT answer costs, by setting.** Heuristic, November 2026. Looking at this card costs nothing. The paste-in check (`effort-cost.paste.md`) costs about one answer, so save it for long threads or unusual requests.

## Energy per answer (Wh): central (low–high)

ChatGPT's Power slider (Nov 2026) has three positions: **Instant**, **Medium** (= Thinking standard below) and **High** (= Thinking high). The light and Pro rows apply to other plans and models.

| Setting | Short reply (~100 tokens) | Paragraph (~300) | Page (~700) | Essay (~1,500) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instant | 0.040 (0.020–0.12) | 0.12 (0.060–0.36) | 0.28 (0.14–0.84) | 0.60 (0.30–1.8) |
| Thinking light | 1.2 (0.35–3.0) | 1.2 (0.35–3.0) | 1.8 (0.49–4.2) | 3.8 (1.0–9.0) |
| Thinking standard (ChatGPT Medium) | 3.0 (1.0–7.5) | 3.0 (1.0–7.5) | 4.2 (1.4–10) | 9.0 (3.0–22) |
| Thinking high (ChatGPT High) | 7.5 (2.5–20) | 7.5 (2.5–20) | 10 (3.5–28) | 22 (7.5–60) |
| Pro | 22 (7.5–75) | 22 (7.5–75) | 31 (10–100) | 68 (22–220) |

**Converting Wh to carbon and water:**
- **Carbon:** multiply by 0.35 to get grams of CO2e.
  - US midday (10 am–4 pm): use 0.30.
  - US evening (4–10 pm): use 0.45.
  - Scenario only, if your answer were computed in your own state: Illinois 0.23, California 0.18.
- **Water:** multiply by 3 to get mL.
  - The range is 1 to 5: about 1 counts cooling only, about 5 includes the water used to generate the electricity.

**Long threads add to every answer**, past roughly 4,000 words (5k tokens):
- 5k–20k tokens: up to about +3 Wh per answer
- 20k–60k tokens: about +12 Wh
- over 60k tokens: about +30 Wh

## Common choices, for an essay-length answer

**Standard → High**
- One answer: about 14 Wh, 4.7 g CO2e, 40 mL more.
  - That's about 1.4 hours of a 10 W LED bulb, or one phone charge.
  - It's about 60 feet of driving.
  - It's a bit more than one sip of water.
- A million answers like this:
  - 14 MWh, about 1.3 US household-years of electricity
  - 4.7 t CO2e, about one car-year
  - 40,000 L, about 37 household-days of water

**Instant → High**
- One answer: about 22 Wh, 7.7 g, 66 mL more.
- A million answers: about 2.1 household-years, 1.7 car-years, 60 household-days of water.

**High → Pro**
- One answer: about 45 Wh, 16 g, 140 mL more.
- A million answers: about 4.3 household-years, 3.4 car-years, 120 household-days of water.

**How to work out "a million answers like this"** (for any gap):
- household-years of electricity = gap Wh × 0.095
- car-years of CO2 = gap g × 0.22
- household-days of water = gap mL × 0.91

**Instant vs Pro for a one-paragraph fact question**
- About 22 Wh more.
- That's roughly 180 times the Instant answer, and buys nothing for a lookup.

## When is more effort worth it?

- **It helps with:**
  - multi-step reasoning and argument
  - close reading of long passages
  - comparing sources
  - checking claims
  - math and code
- **It helps little with:**
  - rephrasing or tone
  - short recall
  - idea lists
  - summaries of text already in the thread
- **The cheapest effective pattern:** bump the effort for the one message that needs it, then switch back.
