I ran auto-eda on `orders.csv` with your cutoff of 12 categories. The report is at `<run dir>/eda_out/orders_eda.html`, with the JSON summary next to it at `<run dir>/eda_out/orders_eda.json`. It covers 500 rows, 7 columns and 8 charts.

Main findings:

- **`amount` has missing values and a long right tail.** 111 of 500 values (22.2%) are missing. It's the only column with gaps. The median is 18.28 but the mean is 29.28, values run from 0.82 to 355.9, and skew is 4.07. A log scale would suit it better than raw values.
- **Returns are rare.** 7.6% of orders have `is_return` = yes (38 of 500). The other 92.4% are no.
- **`constant_flag` is "A" in every row,** so it tells you nothing and you can drop it. `order_id` is a unique ID; it's profiled but not charted.
- **Regions are evenly spread** (W 28.4%, E 26.6%, N 23.0%, S 22.0%). `amount` and `qty` are essentially uncorrelated (r = 0.05). `qty` is roughly uniform from 1 to 39 (median 21). The orders are hourly and run from 2026-01-01 00:00 to 2026-01-21 19:00.

About the cutoff: the 12-category setting didn't change any classification here. `region` has 4 values, so it's a category under either cutoff. `qty` has 39 distinct values, so it's still charted as a numeric histogram rather than a bar chart.
