## Positions
**A (steelman).** Remote setups remove commuting and interruptions, and developers who control their environment can sustain longer focused work, so output could rise. **B (steelman).** Remote setups remove ambient coordination, slow questions and onboarding, and make it harder to notice blocked colleagues, so output could fall. The two directly conflict on direction of effect but could both be true in different team settings, which would make the blanket statements in each too strong. They share the premise that the effect is large enough to matter.

## Framing notes
- Both positions say "fully remote ... software companies", a very broad category.
- "Productivity" is undefined in both; output, quality and speed need not move together.
