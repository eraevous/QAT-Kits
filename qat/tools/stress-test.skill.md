# StressTest

## Purpose
Apply constructive, adversarial scrutiny to an idea, without substituting reflexive contrarianism for reasoning. Separate observations, plausible concerns, and unknowns.

Use a sibling skill instead when: comparing two positions (truth-weight), looking for what is absent or inverting the frame (negative-space), or critiquing prose style (frame-shift).

## Invocation
If invoked with no idea attached, give a one-line summary, list the lenses below, and ask for the idea plus (optionally) domain, stakes, and depth. Default to a compact report; expand when stakes or the user ask for it.

## Inputs
An idea, design, argument, plan, or prior response. Optional: domain, stakes, evidence, and depth.

## The core mini stress-test
Answer these (skip any that genuinely don't apply, and say so):
1. What is the idea secretly assuming?
2. Where might it be missing context or bias?
3. What's one way this could backfire?
4. How would you tweak it for more clarity or impact?
5. What is being assumed that feels *true* but might just be *comfortable*?
6. What part of this might be a reaction to pain rather than a neutral insight?
7. Who would push back on this, and might they have a point?
8. If the idea were reversed entirely, what might be learned?
9. What's one *uncomfortable* tweak that would make it more resilient?

Questions 5 and 6 probe the user's own relationship to the idea. The user is asking for that, so engage with them, but pose what you notice as questions or possibilities grounded in the text ("this framing reads as reactive to X; is it?"), not as diagnoses of motive. If nothing in the material supports a reading, say so rather than inventing one.

## Lenses (apply when relevant)
- **Reversal Lens (Dialectical Flip):** Assume the opposite is true. What does that teach? Where would it succeed? Who would thrive under that assumption, and why?
- **Sharpen & Refine:** Strip the idea to its essence. What is its clearest, most precise form? What is noise or emotional cushioning? How could it be tightened for maximum impact?
- **Ethical Echo:** Second-order ethical effects. Who benefits, who is harmed, what unintended norms or incentives does it set?
- **Application Stress:** 3 use cases where it would shine, 3 where it would fail, and 1 that would mutate it into something dangerous or strange (when useful, not mechanically).
- **Bias Climber:** Where is the idea too shaped by the user's own perspective, experience, or trauma? Help them climb above their vantage point and look down from new angles. (Same rule as questions 5–6: offer angles and questions, don't assert psychology.)

## Workflow
1. Restate the core claim or intended result neutrally. Do not manufacture assumptions.
2. Surface implicit premises and sort them into **necessary premises**, **unverified premises**, and **value judgments**.
3. Identify missing context, relevant stakeholders, and credible opposing views; run the core questions, then the lenses that add something.
4. Identify one or two high-leverage revisions and state what evidence would change the evaluation.

## Output
**Core idea**; **Assumptions** (sorted); **Blind spots and backfire risks**; **Strongest counterargument**; **Lens findings**; **The uncomfortable tweak**; **Tests or next evidence**. Vary sections with scope.

## Checks
- Do not invent facts or project motives the material doesn't support.
- Distinguish genuine contradictions from alternative priorities.
- Avoid forced criticism when a premise is supported.
- If an empirical claim is involved, say what verification is actually available.
- StressTest can be applied to your own prior outputs and assumptions when the user asks.
